How it works

Three steps. The last one takes seconds.

The whole app is built around one moment: you are parked, it is dark, and you need to know which part of the car to reach into.

01

Set up your vehicle once

Pick the layout closest to what you drive and the app builds the zones for you — front, middle, trunk, or whatever you name them. Rename them, add your own, drag to reorder.

02

Load by scanning

Point the camera at a shipping label and tap once. The app reads the recipient's name and files it under the zone you picked. No signal needed — it works in a dead-zone warehouse.

03

Deliver in seconds

Search a name, see the zone in large type, grab the package, check it off. No scrolling a list, no second-guessing which bag it went in.

Why it exists

The paper note on your dashboard was the best tool available. That is the problem.

Every driver works out some version of the same system — a scribbled list, a mental map of the back seat, a guess. It works right up until a stop has two packages in two different places, or the handwriting stops making sense at 5 a.m.

Scan & Sort is that system, done properly. It does not plan your route or tell you where to drive — your delivery app already does that, and doing it twice just means two apps disagreeing with each other. This does the one job the route app has never done: remembering where in your car each package actually is.

Features

Built around how the job actually goes

Split-stop warning

Two packages for one person, in different spots? You get a warning before you walk away with only one of them.

Boxes and envelopes

Each package is labelled by type, so you know whether you are reaching for a box or something flat that slid under a seat.

Works fully offline

Dead-zone warehouses, basement garages, airplane mode. Nothing the app does requires a connection — there is no server to reach.

Night mode

For pre-dawn loading, when a white screen at the back of a van is the last thing your eyes want.

Undo on everything

Wrong zone, wrong name, wrong tap. Every action can be taken back, so a mistake costs a second rather than a stop.

English and Spanish

The full app, not a half-translated menu — including every button you touch while you are loading.

Fix a misread name

Scuffed label? Tap the entry and type it, or use your keyboard's microphone. Corrections stick for the whole trip.

Survives a dead battery

Your trip saves continuously. Close the app, restart the phone, run it flat — open it again and the trip is waiting where you left it.

Your own zones

A sedan, a cargo van and a hatchback are not the same vehicle. Add a zone mid-route if you need one.

Privacy

You are handling other people's names. So is this app.

That is worth being specific about rather than vague, so here is exactly what happens and what does not.

  • It records a name only. The app never stores or transmits a street address — not once, not anywhere.
  • Everything stays on your phone. There is no server and no account, so there is no copy for anyone to see, hand over, or lose.
  • Label photos are deleted immediately. They are read on the device and never uploaded or saved.
  • Finished trips delete themselves after seven days, by default.
  • It never touches your delivery account. No credentials, ever. It reads a printed label with the camera, the same way you read it with your eyes.

The honest other half: because there is no server, there is also no backup. Your trips live on that phone only — lose it and they are gone. That is the same fact as “nobody can leak your customers' names,” seen from the other side. Read the full privacy policy →
